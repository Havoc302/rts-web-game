Current task: Fix crime being driven by the wrong "unemployment" metric, which masked the tax rate's effect on growth.

- [done] Root cause: CrimeManager used `1 - stats.employmentRate` (jobsFilled / totalJobsProvided) as its unemployment signal. That's a job-vacancy-fill ratio, not resident unemployment — in a city with abundant jobs relative to population it reads as near-zero (e.g. 0.01), which the crime scaler (2.5x) blew up into near-guaranteed crime every tick regardless of tax rate, capping tile crime at 10 and permanently zeroing growthScore via the crime growth penalty. That made it look like tax rate no longer affected growth (both 0% and 100% tax stalled at growthScore 0), while employment rate still visibly moved with tax since jobsMultiplier is computed independently.
- [done] Renamed `CRIME_CONFIG.UNEMPLOYMENT_CRIME_SCALER` to `JOB_SCARCITY_CRIME_SCALER` and changed CrimeManager to drive crime probability off resident job scarcity: `(totalEmployablePopulation - jobsFilled) / totalEmployablePopulation` (residents who want work but can't get any, because there aren't enough jobs), instead of the jobs-side employment rate. A city with more jobs than residents now reads as zero scarcity (low baseline crime only) even before every job slot is filled.
